# Spatiotemporal Representation Fusion-based Progressive Diagnosis-to-Prognosis Curriculum Learning for Disease-Specific Survival Prediction from Multi-View Cine CMR


A spatiotemporal representation fusion framework with curriculum-inspired progressive diagnosis-to-prognosis learning for disease-specific survival prediction from real-world multiview cine CMR. 

The framework comprises two consecutive stages. The first stage performs diagnostic phenotype learning: rather than dividing the imaging data by disease at the beginning of representation learning, the complete NC/HCM/DCM cine CMR cohort is first used for diagnostic classification. A spatiotemporal representation network built around a Video Swin Transformer processes multiview cine CMR and learns cardiomyopathy-related structural and motion representations from the broader imaging cohort under explicit phenotype supervision. In the second stage, the imaging representations established during diagnostic phenotype learning are retained for separate HCM- and DCM-specific prognostic modeling, where follow-up times and event indicators provide supervision for outcome-risk estimation. This progressive diagnosis-to-prognosis learning process is inspired by the easy-to-difficult principle of curriculum learning: the model first learns cardiomyopathy-related imaging phenotypes through diagnostic classification, where supervision is explicit and available across the broader imaging cohort, and subsequently progresses to disease-specific time-to-event prediction under more limited survival supervision. In this way, the framework makes fuller use of the available imaging data for phenotype learning while allowing prognostic risk modeling to be performed separately within HCM and DCM.
